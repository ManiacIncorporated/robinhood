# Research Notes: How Distillation Datasets Are Actually Curated

A compilation of techniques used by DeepSeek, MiniMax, Qwen, and open-source
projects to curate high-quality reasoning distillation datasets. These findings
informed the design of robinhood.

---

## 1. The DeepSeek R1 Recipe (800K verified traces)

DeepSeek R1's distilled models (R1-Distill-Qwen-7B/14B/32B, R1-Distill-Llama-8B/70B)
are the most widely used distilled reasoning models. Their process:

- **800,000 reasoning traces** selected from a much larger pool generated by
  the full R1 model (trained with RL, not distillation itself)
- Each trace is **verified for correctness**:
  - Math problems: checked against reference answers
  - Code problems: validated with test cases
  - Other tasks: scored by reward models
- **Semantic deduplication** to remove near-duplicate traces
- **Test set contamination removal** to ensure clean evaluation
- Pure **SFT** on these 800K traces, no RL on the distilled models

**Key insight:** The distilled models don't do any RL themselves. All the
reasoning quality comes from the *data quality* of the 800K traces.

**Source:** DeepSeek-R1 Technical Report (2025)

## 2. MiniMax M2: Verifiable vs Non-Verifiable Pipelines

MiniMax published the most detailed public account of reasoning data curation
in their "What Makes Good Reasoning Data" blog post. Their approach:

### Two separate data pipelines

| Pipeline | Tasks | Quality Signal | Difficulty Metric |
|----------|-------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Verifiable** | Math, Code | Automated (test cases, reference answers) | Pass rate |
| **Non-Verifiable** | Logical reasoning, Science, Creative, Instruction following | Rules + LLM-as-judge | Complexity score |

### Key findings

1. **CoT quality matters more than length.** Overly brief CoTs cause the model
   to skip steps and become overconfident. But excessive redundancy hurts too.
   The sweet spot is logically complete reasoning without repetition.

2. **Format diversity prevents benchmark overfitting.** If all your training
   data uses the same reasoning format, the model overfits to that format and
   generalizes poorly. Vary the structure of reasoning traces.

3. **Math and code data improve everything.** Even for non-STEM tasks, including
   math and code reasoning data improves instruction following and general
   capabilities. These domains teach structured thinking that transfers.

4. **Harder queries are more valuable.** Per training token, complex problems
   contribute more to capability than easy ones. Bias your data distribution
   toward difficulty.

5. **Data cleaning is critical.** Use rules + LLM-as-judge to eliminate:
   - Hallucinations in the reasoning trace
   - Instruction-following failures
   - Logical errors / non-sequiturs
   - Premature conclusions

6. **Scale helps, but only with quality.** When quality and diversity standards
   are met, more data consistently helps via:
   - More unique queries
   - Multiple answers per query
   - Multi-epoch training
   - Cross-domain data mixing

**Source:** [What Makes Good Reasoning Data](https://huggingface.co/blog/MiniMax-AI/what-makes-good-reasoning-data) (MiniMax, 2025)

## 3. OpenThoughts: 1000 Controlled Experiments

The OpenThoughts project ran over 1,000 controlled experiments ablating each
stage of the distillation pipeline. This is the most rigorous empirical study
of what matters in reasoning data curation.

### Pipeline stages tested

1. Question sourcing
2. Question mixing (combining sources)
3. Question filtering
4. Multi-answer sampling (16 answers per question from teacher)
5. Answer filtering
6. Teacher model selection

### Surprising findings

- **Source concentration beats diversity.** Selecting from 1-2 high-quality
  question sources outperforms sampling from 8-16 sources. Quality of the
  question source matters more than breadth.

- **Better benchmark models aren't better teachers.** QwQ-32B produces better
  distillation data than DeepSeek-R1 despite lower benchmark scores. The best
  teacher is the one whose reasoning style transfers best, not the one with the
  highest accuracy.

- **Multi-answer sampling is huge.** Sampling 16 answers per question from the
  teacher and keeping the best one increases dataset size 16x while also
  improving quality. This is essentially rejection sampling at scale.

- **Answer filtering doesn't help much.** Surprisingly, filtering answers
  (beyond basic correctness checks) provided no significant improvement.

- **Difficulty filtering matters.** Removing questions that are too easy or
  too hard (for the teacher) improves the dataset. The best questions are in
  an intermediate difficulty range where the teacher sometimes gets it right
  and sometimes doesn't.

**Result:** OpenThinker3-7B trained on 1.2M examples achieves 53% on AIME 2025,
beating DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B by 15.3 points.

**Source:** [OpenThoughts3 Blog](https://www.openthoughts.ai/blog/ot3) (2025)

## 4. LIMO: 817 Examples Is Enough

The most extreme demonstration that data quality dominates quantity:

- **817 carefully curated examples** (yes, less than a thousand)
- Fine-tuned on Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct
- **57.1% on AIME** (vs 6.5% for previous SFT approaches)
- **94.8% on MATH** (vs 59.2% previously)
- Uses **1% of the data** required by other approaches

### The "Less-Is-More Reasoning Hypothesis"

Sophisticated reasoning can emerge from minimal but precisely selected
demonstrations that function as **cognitive templates** — showing the model
how to activate and compose its pre-trained knowledge, rather than teaching
it new knowledge.

**Implication for robinhood:** You may not need millions of traces. A few
hundred extremely well-chosen, verified examples targeting the specific
skills you care about could be more effective than a massive noisy dataset.

**Source:** [LIMO: Less is More for Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03387) (2025)

## 5. Light-R1: Curriculum Learning

Light-R1 showed that **progressive difficulty scheduling** during training
significantly improves reasoning distillation:

### Three-stage curriculum

1. **Stage 1: SFT** on reasoning traces, starting with easier examples and
   progressively increasing difficulty
2. **Stage 2: DPO** (Direct Preference Optimization) using pairs of good
   and bad reasoning traces
3. **Stage 3: RL** (Reinforcement Learning) for further refinement

### Key result

Light-R1-14B-DS achieves 74.0 on AIME24 and 60.2 on AIME25, surpassing
many 32B models and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B — a model 5x its size.

Training cost: ~$1,000 on 12 x H800 machines for 6 hours.

**Implication for robinhood:** When generating your dataset, tag examples
by difficulty level (this is what skill files do). Train in order of
increasing difficulty rather than random shuffle.

**Source:** [Light-R1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10460) (2025)

## 6. s1: Budget Forcing and 1000 Examples

The s1 project showed two important things:

### Curated dataset of 1,000 examples (s1K)

Selected using three criteria:
1. **Difficulty** — hard enough to require extended reasoning
2. **Diversity** — covers different problem types
3. **Quality** — verified correct with clean reasoning traces

### Budget forcing at inference time

A technique that controls test-time compute by:
- Forcefully **terminating** thinking when budget is exceeded
- **Extending** thinking by appending "Wait" tokens when the model tries to
  stop too early, forcing it to double-check

This simple trick takes a model from 50% to 57% on AIME24 with no additional
training.

**Implication for robinhood:** Consider including "Wait, let me verify..."
patterns in your reasoning traces. Models trained on traces that include
self-correction steps learn to self-correct at inference time.

**Source:** [s1: Simple Test-Time Scaling](https://simplescaling.github.io/) (2025)

## 7. DLCoT: Structured Decomposition of Long CoT

The DLCoT framework addresses a subtle problem: long chain-of-thought traces
from large models often contain **dead ends, redundancies, and errors** that
hurt distillation quality.

### Three-step cleanup

1. **Segmentation** — decompose the long CoT into logical segments
2. **Simplification** — remove dead-end reasoning paths and redundant steps
3. **Optimization** — fix intermediate error states

### Cross-family distillation problem

DLCoT found that distillation effectiveness **degrades substantially** when
the teacher and student are from different model families (e.g., QwQ teacher
-> Llama student). Structured decomposition helps bridge this gap.

**Implication for robinhood:** Raw reasoning traces from Claude may not be
optimal for training a Qwen or Llama student. Consider post-processing
traces to remove redundancies and dead ends.

**Source:** [DLCoT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16385) (2025)

## 8. Reinforcement Distillation (REDI): Don't Throw Away Bad Examples

Standard rejection sampling discards incorrect reasoning traces. REDI shows
this is wasteful:

### Two-stage approach

1. **Stage 1:** SFT on positive (correct) traces — standard distillation
2. **Stage 2:** Refinement using *both* positive and negative traces with a
   reference-free contrastive loss (outperforms DPO and SimPO)

### Result

Qwen-REDI-1.5B trained on **131K examples** achieves 83.1% on MATH-500,
matching models trained on 800K proprietary examples.

**Implication for robinhood:** When doing rejection sampling (generating
multiple answers and filtering), save the rejected answers. They're useful
for a DPO/REDI stage after initial SFT.

**Source:** [REDI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.xxxxx) (2025)

## Summary: What To Do In Practice

Based on all the above research, here's the practical playbook:

### Data Collection

1. **Use skill files** to target specific capabilities rather than collecting
   random traces
2. **Sample multiple answers per prompt** (8-16x) and keep the best verified
   one (rejection sampling)
3. **Focus on intermediate difficulty** — not too easy, not impossible
4. **Math and code data improve everything**, even non-STEM tasks

### Data Curation

5. **Verify correctness** — test cases for code, reference answers for math,
   LLM-as-judge for open-ended tasks
6. **Clean the reasoning traces** — remove dead ends, redundancies, and
   errors (DLCoT-style)
7. **Maintain format diversity** — don't let all traces look the same
8. **Deduplicate** semantically, not just exact match

### Training

9. **Quality over quantity** — 1K perfect examples can beat 100K noisy ones
   (LIMO, s1)
10. **Curriculum learning** — train easy-to-hard, not random order (Light-R1)
11. **Save negative examples** for a DPO stage after SFT (REDI)
12. **Include self-correction patterns** in traces — "Wait, let me check..."
    (s1 budget forcing)

### Teacher Selection

13. **Best benchmarks ≠ best teacher** — test multiple teachers and pick
    the one whose reasoning style transfers best to your student
14. **Cross-family distillation is harder** — prefer teachers from the same
    model family when possible, or use DLCoT-style trace cleanup
